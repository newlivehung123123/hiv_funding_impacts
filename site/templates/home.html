What happens to HIV services when the funding is withdrawn

The United States has restructured the country's global health assistance under the America First Global Health Strategy. Our project brings together the published record of what followed the restructuring, including donor funding, epidemic estimates, treatment coverage and the records published by national HIV programmes. The aim of our project is to measure how the restructuring affects HIV programmes in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Southeast Asia, South Asia, East Asia and the Pacific, Eastern and Southern Africa, Western and Central Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, and Eastern Europe and Central Asia are covered, and we will extend the coverage region by region until all {{ regions|length|count }} LMIC regions are covered.

Reconciliation

Most agreement between sources is republication, not corroboration.

UNAIDS, the WHO Global Health Observatory and the World Bank largely publish the same modelled estimate. When UNAIDS, the WHO and the World Bank agree, the agreement follows from the shared estimate. Counting agreement between copies of a single estimate as independent confirmation would create confidence that the data do not support.

Of {{ recon.n_total_fmt }} pairwise comparisons across the panel, {{ recon.derived_pct }}% fall between sources that share an underlying estimate. Only {{ recon.independent_fmt }} comparisons, on {{ recon.n_independent_pairs|count }} source pairs, are between separate measurement systems, where the data differences carry meaningful information.

Every classification is inferred from the data and published with the figures that support the classification, namely the number of matched figures, the median relative difference, the share of matches that agree within 2 per cent and the share in which the same source gives the smaller figure. A reader can check any classification against the published figures.

How the panel is built

Feasibility in {{ live|length|count }} regions

Structured sources only

Figures come from machine-readable feeds and from published tables. Prose is never parsed for numbers, and no language model is used at any step of the data collection. Where a country does not publish in a usable form, the site says so instead of estimating. Reading structured sources only is designed to maximise data accuracy and to avoid data contamination.

Read at source where possible

National programme reporting is the primary record, and the international and donor bodies that compile figures across many countries provide the cross-check. National sources are read directly in 35 countries, namely Thailand, Cambodia and the Philippines in Southeast Asia, Bangladesh, India, the Maldives, Nepal and Sri Lanka in South Asia, China, Mongolia, North Korea, Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands and Vanuatu in East Asia and the Pacific, South Africa in Eastern and Southern Africa, Nigeria, Cameroon, Niger, Guinea and Chad in Western and Central Africa, Argentina, Belize, Bolivia, Haiti, Nicaragua, Paraguay and Peru in Latin America and the Caribbean, and Armenia, Belarus, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Montenegro, Tajikistan and Ukraine in Eastern Europe and Central Asia. Our project also reads community-led monitoring reports for Indonesia and India. Every other country appears through international and donor reporting alone, and every country page names the sources used for the country.

Uncertainty is kept

Modelled estimates come with uncertainty intervals, and the panel stores and draws the intervals. Reporting periods that are still open are marked, because plotting a partial year as a complete year would understate the total for the year when only part of the year's funding has been reported.
